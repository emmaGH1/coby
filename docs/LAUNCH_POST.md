## option 1: single post (pairs with the demo video)

i built coby because traditional productivity tools were actively making my adhd worse.

whenever my thoughts got chaotic, everyone told me to "just use notion" or build a second brain. but opening notion meant staring at empty databases, relations, tags, and formatting just to get three quick things out of my head before 5pm. by the time i configured a template, my working memory was gone and executive dysfunction took over.

i didn't need another system to manage or another dashboard to maintain. i just needed a way to unload my brain before things slipped.

so i built coby.

coby is a calm, voice-first companion for when you are carrying too much in your head. 

you just talk or brain dump whatever is bouncing around in your mind in messy, unstructured language. coby quietly extracts the tasks, dates, and reminders, gives you a calm receipt to review, and holds them for you.

the most important part for me: it never drowns you in a wall of 40 tasks. it only surfaces one single thing to do "now" and at most two things for "next". when you are ready to work, you tap into focus mode and handle one thing at a time with zero distractions.

no complex setups. no endless tag management. just talk, let it hold the weight, and focus on what matters.

built this for the nextgen award at @revenuecat #shipaton 2026. check out the quick demo below to see it in action.

github: https://github.com/emmaGH1/coby

would love to hear your thoughts, feedback, or what you use when your brain feels completely full.

### tweet 2 (introducing coby)
so i built coby: a calm, voice-first companion for people who carry too much in their head.

the core loop is dead simple:
1. tap and talk (or text) your messy brain dump
2. coby quietly extracts the tasks, dates, and reminders
3. you get a clean receipt to verify, then it holds them safely

### tweet 3 (why it actually helps adhd)
the problem with most task managers is the dread of opening an infinite list of 50 tasks.

coby refuses to do that to you. it only surfaces:
- exactly one thing for "now"
- at most two things for "next"

tap it, enter focus mode, finish it, and breathe. no guilt, no overwhelm, no cognitive friction.
